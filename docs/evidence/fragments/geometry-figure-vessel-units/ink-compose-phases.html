Compose two machine states into one figure

geometry_figure_mpl (imas_ink/figures.py) takes an optional ax. When given one it draws into that axes and returns ax.get_figure() without calling plt.subplots, so a caller places two machine states on axes it lays out itself; when omitted it builds its own figure exactly as before. InkStyle (imas_ink/style.py) gains wall_linestyle (default "solid"), applied by _render_wall_mpl (imas_ink/mpl.py) and _render_wall_alt (imas_ink/alt.py) to the limiter units and the vacuum-vessel shells. The altair renderer maps the style name to a Vega-Lite strokeDash through a module-level WALL_STROKE_DASH, leaving no second style vocabulary.

Measure: tests/unit/test_vessel_units.py. Two new figure tests plus one altair test and a solid control. Drawing two geometries into two axes of one figure through ax leaves plt.get_fignums() unchanged, returns the caller's figure and axes, and each axes holds its own geometry's wall lines (one limiter unit on one, three lines — limiter plus two shells — on the other). Drawing a geometry with wall_linestyle="dashed" makes all three wall lines dashed, against a control that the default draws them solid. The altair test asserts the mark carries strokeDash=[6, 4] when the style is dashed.
